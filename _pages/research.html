Efficient Multimodal Foundation Models Ongoing

A Mamba-Transformer hybrid for unified multimodal understanding and generation

Unified multimodal models are usually dense Transformers, so their attention cost and KV cache grow with every token of context. Our model replaces most attention layers with Mamba-2 state-space layers. Each block pairs one Transformer layer with five Mamba-2 layers whose projections form a modality-aware mixture of experts: a parameter-free mask sends text and image tokens to separate expert pools, and a learned router picks one expert within the pool. Images are read through a dual visual tokenizer (VQGAN codes plus SigLIP features) and generated autoregressively as discrete tokens. Understanding and generation are trained jointly from the start, with no separate alignment stage.

  • 1.7B parameters, 0.39B active per token, with top-1 routing over 8 experts per layer.
  • GenEval 0.70 for text-to-image generation, within 0.03 of Janus-Pro-1B and Show-o2 with about a quarter of their active parameters.
  • Long context on a single GPU. At 1M tokens, prefill is 15.8× faster than Qwen3.5-2B (21.2 s vs 334.7 s) with 26% lower peak memory. Full-attention unified models (Show-o, Janus-Pro) run out of memory beyond 256K tokens. At 128K, decoding runs at 141 tokens/s, 4.6× faster than Qwen3.5-2B.
  • Scaling up: a 6.3B-parameter version (1.59B active) is in training.

World Models Ongoing

World models learn to simulate how an environment evolves and responds to actions, a foundation for embodied AI, interactive media and planning. I founded and lead a research initiative on world models with three threads:

  • Long-horizon video generation that stays consistent as a scene unfolds.
  • Real-time interactive world generation that responds to user actions as it renders.
  • 3D scene generation for explorable, geometrically consistent environments.

The initiative also works backwards to infrastructure, defining what next-generation training and serving systems need to support these models.

Agentic AI Ongoing

Prefix caching for hybrid LLMs In progress

Hybrid LLMs interleave attention with linear-attention or state-space layers. To reuse a cached prefix, a serving engine needs both the attention KV for the prefix and the recurrent state at exactly its end. Paged engines such as vLLM manage KV block by block but keep recurrent state only at a few positions chosen by KV rules, so a KV hit is often unusable and the shared prefix is recomputed. Our approach makes the unit of caching an anchor: the recurrent state at a position, bound to the KV before it, and registered, looked up and evicted as one entry. A lightweight scoring head on the frozen model decides where to place anchors.

  • Replaying 5,076 recorded calls from five multi-agent frameworks on Qwen3.5-4B in vLLM, 7.9% of all prefill tokens were recomputed despite a KV hit.
  • In our vLLM implementation, generated tokens and log-probabilities after restoring from anchors were bitwise identical to recomputing from scratch on all 409 validation requests.

AI for Acoustics

Deep learning for active noise control (ANC) in earbuds and vehicle cabins, with collaborators at Nanyang Technological University. Classical adaptive ANC converges slowly and struggles with fast-changing noise, so our work replaces or initializes the adaptive loop with learned components:

  • Selective and generative fixed-filter ANC: neural networks choose or synthesize control filters without adding delay.
  • Meta-learned initialization of control filters and secondary paths (APSIPA ASC 2025 Best Workshop Paper; ICASSP 2026).
  • Continual learning and Transformer-based end-to-end filter generation.
  • Prompt-guided ANC that cancels noise while preserving sound from chosen directions.
  • Spatial audio: robust stereo 3-D sound event localization and detection.
